## Can a Dighi Cool Dhaka? The Answer Is Smaller Than We Think

In Bogura, a client once asked me to remove the courtyard from his house plan. He wanted more floor area instead. That courtyard was the only open space where air could move and light could enter.

The same choice is made at city scale in Dhaka, again and again. A dighi is filled in for one more plot. A canal is covered for one more road. Each time the gain looks small and reasonable. Added together, the loss is now measurable.

A 2026 report by the Change Initiative, "Dhaka Without Nature?", looked at 44 years of satellite data. Between 1980 and 2024, Dhaka lost 61 percent of its waterbodies. Waterbody cover fell to 4.8 percent of the city. Each person in Dhaka now has 1.43 square metres of waterbody, against a recommended minimum of 4.5. In the same period, land surface temperature rose by 3 to 5 degrees Celsius. No area of the city now stays under 30 degrees.

So the question matters. Can a dighi cool Dhaka? The honest answer from research is: a little, on its own. Much more, if we design around it properly.

## What a Pond Actually Does to the Air

It is easy to assume water always cools a city. The research is more careful than that.

Jacobs and colleagues, in Urban Climate in 2020, simulated small urban water bodies in the Netherlands. They found the cooling effect on afternoon air temperature was typically 0.2 degrees Celsius, with a maximum of 0.6 degrees. The physiological equivalent temperature, which is how hot a person actually feels, fell typically by 0.6 degrees and at most by 1.9 degrees. At night, the water could even warm the air slightly.

That does not mean ponds are useless. It means a bare pond in a concrete yard is not a cooling machine. It is one part of a system.

Syafii and colleagues, in a scale model study in Sustainable Cities and Society in 2017, tested ponds inside an urban canyon. A larger pond surface gave a greater cooling effect. Ponds set parallel to the prevailing wind worked best. But the same study warned that a pond also raises humidity, which can make people feel less comfortable if the air cannot move.

A later parametric study by Syafii and colleagues in 2021 reached a similar conclusion. The shape, size and position of a pond matter, and ponds spread across a neighbourhood cool a wider area than one pond in one place.

## The Trees Around the Water Do Most of the Work

Here is the finding that changes the plan. In the same Dutch simulations, the best designs combined water with shade trees, fountains or water mist. The feeling of heat, measured as PET, could fall by as much as 7 degrees at 3 in the afternoon, but that difference came mainly from the shade of the trees, not from the water itself.

So the rule is simple. Do not save a dighi and then pave its banks and cut its trees. Save the dighi with its trees, its walking path and its open sky. Water plus shade is the combination that cools people. Water alone is not.

## A Pond Is Also a Place, Not Only a Cooler

Cooling is only one reason to keep a dighi. People need somewhere to go.

Lu and colleagues, in Frontiers in Psychology in 2025, surveyed 532 visitors at freshwater blue spaces in Hengshui, China. What people saw, heard and could touch mattered for their wellbeing. A clean view, a calm soundscape and easy access to the water all helped. Smell, on its own, did not.

Wang and colleagues, in Frontiers in Public Health in October 2025, surveyed 429 people in five urban parks with blue spaces in Xian, China. They found that the quality of the blue space mattered for wellbeing, and the benefit came partly through cleaner air and partly through exercise. A neglected pond behind a fence does not give that benefit. A cared for pond with a path, a bench and clean water does.

Smith and colleagues, in Public Health in Practice in 2025, brought researchers and practitioners together to build a whole system plan for blue spaces. Their plan rests on four mechanisms: physical activity, social interaction, a healthy environment, and lower stress. A dighi can serve all four, but only if people can actually reach it and use it.

That is why a fenced, dirty pond is a missed chance. The water is there. The benefit is locked away.

## What Dhaka Has Already Lost

The loss is not only about heat. Waterbodies and wetlands hold rainwater. When they are filled, the rain has nowhere to go.

Dialogue Earth reported that of the 56 canals that once flowed in Dhaka, only 12 remain. A 2012 study by the Centre for Environmental and Geographic Information Services found Dhaka had lost 75 percent of its perennial wetlands in four decades. The Change Initiative report puts the same story in numbers for 1980 to 2024: built up area grew sevenfold, tree cover fell from 21.6 percent to 11.6 percent, and waterbodies fell by 61 percent.

The burden is not shared equally. The report notes that central areas such as Wari, Bangshal and Kotwali are almost entirely deprived of trees and waterbodies, while peripheral areas still retain some. Poorer neighbourhoods bear the heat.

## Five Practical Steps

1. Stop filling what is left. Every remaining dighi, canal and wetland is now scarce infrastructure, not empty land. Protect it first, and argue about the plot later.
2. Keep the trees with the water. A dighi without shade cools the air by less than one degree. The same dighi under trees can feel several degrees cooler to a person standing there.
3. Make the water reachable. A path, a bench, steps down to the water and clean water matter as much as the pond itself. If people cannot sit by it, walk by it or touch it, most of the health benefit is lost.
4. Spread small water across the neighbourhood. One large pond in one place helps one place. Several ponds, rain gardens and restored canal edges, spread across a neighbourhood and set along the wind, cool a wider area.
5. Clean the water before counting the benefit. A polluted pond is a health risk, not a health asset. Cleaning, and keeping sewage and waste out, has to come before any claim about cooling or wellbeing.

## A Healthy City Needs Its Water Back

A dighi will not cool Dhaka on its own. The research is clear about that, and it is better to say so plainly than to promise a miracle.

But a dighi with trees, clean water and a path is something else. It cools the people standing near it, holds rainwater, gives children a place to play and older people a place to sit, and gives a neighbourhood a centre.

Dhaka has already lost 61 percent of its waterbodies. What is left is worth more than the plot it sits on. The next choice, for one pond or one canal, is the same choice my client faced in Bogura. More floor area now, or air, water and shade for years.

## Frequently Asked Questions

How much does a small pond cool the air?
Typically by 0.2 to 0.6 degrees Celsius in the afternoon, according to Jacobs and colleagues in Urban Climate in 2020. How hot a person feels can fall by up to 1.9 degrees.

Why do trees matter more than the water?
In the same simulations, shade trees around the water cut the felt temperature by up to 7 degrees at 3 in the afternoon. The water alone could not do that.

Does a pond make the air more humid?
Yes, and that can reduce comfort if the air cannot move. Ponds work best when they are set along the wind, with open air movement and shade.

How much waterbody has Dhaka lost?
Dhaka lost 61 percent of its waterbodies between 1980 and 2024, according to the Change Initiative report "Dhaka Without Nature?". Cover fell to 4.8 percent of the city.

What makes a pond good for health?
Clean water, a good view, calm sound, easy access and a place to walk and sit. Lu and colleagues in 2025 found sight, sound and touch all mattered for wellbeing. Wang and colleagues in 2025 found quality mattered through cleaner air and exercise.
